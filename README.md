
# Healthcare SQL Analysis

## Project Overview

This project uses **PostgreSQL** to analyze a synthetic healthcare dataset generated with **Synthea**. The goal was to build practical SQL skills while exploring patient demographics, healthcare utilization, encounter patterns, and claim costs.

The project follows a series of analytical questions, from building a relational database and validating data to examining relationships between patient utilization and cumulative healthcare costs.

## Tools & Technologies

- **PostgreSQL 16** – Database management and SQL analysis
- **pgAdmin 4** – Database administration and query execution
- **SQL** – Data cleaning, aggregation, CTEs, and statistical analysis
- **Synthea** – Synthetic healthcare data generation
- **Git & GitHub** – Version control and project documentation

## Dataset & Preparation

The project uses synthetic patient and encounter records generated by Synthea.

- 1,798 patient records imported
- 103,335 encounter records loaded into a staging table
- 103,214 valid encounters inserted into the final encounters table

### Data Cleaning & Quality Checks

Before analysis, I performed data validation and preparation:

- Identified and corrected malformed encounter records, removing four invalid rows.
- Investigated 121 encounter records referencing patient IDs absent from the patient table.
- Used a staging table to validate encounter data before inserting records into the relational table.
- Established primary keys for patient and encounter records and a foreign key relationship between the tables.

The final database contains two primary analytical tables: `patients` and `encounters`.

## Project Phases & Analysis

### Phase 1: Database Setup & Data Preparation

- Installed and configured PostgreSQL and pgAdmin.
- Generated and imported Synthea healthcare data.
- Created relational tables and established primary and foreign key relationships.
- Validated data integrity before analysis.

### Phase 2: Patient Population Analysis

Explored patient demographics and population characteristics.

**Key Findings:**

- 1,798 total patients.
- Gender distribution was approximately 51% female and 49% male.
- Approximately 82% of records identified patients as White.
- The largest age group was patients aged 65 and older (22.3%).
- Income and healthcare coverage showed virtually no linear correlation (r = -0.006).

### Phase 3: Healthcare Utilization Analysis

Examined encounter frequency, encounter types, and patient utilization patterns.

**Key Findings:**

- 103,214 valid encounters.
- Average of 58.91 encounters per patient; median of 36.
- Ambulatory encounters represented approximately 54.1% of all encounters.
- Identified the 20 patients with the highest encounter frequency.
- Compared encounter-type distributions between the highest-utilization patients and the overall population.

The top 20 highest-utilization patients had a much greater share of ambulatory encounters (86.93%) compared with the overall population (54.08%).

### Phase 4: Healthcare Cost Analysis

Analyzed claim costs, payer coverage, and cost differences across encounter types.

**Key Findings:**

- Total claim costs: $285.1 million.
- Average claim cost per encounter: $2,762.30.
- Median claim cost per encounter: $914.78.
- Inpatient encounters had the highest average claim cost ($30,400.75).
- Ambulatory encounters accounted for the largest total claim cost ($160.4 million).
- Payer coverage varied by encounter type, ranging from approximately 44.6% for wellness encounters to 81.5% for home encounters.

Also identified the 20 patients with the highest cumulative claim costs and examined differences in payer coverage.

### Phase 5: Patient Utilization & Cost Analysis

Investigated how encounter frequency relates to cumulative claim costs.

Patients were classified into exploratory utilization groups:

| Group | Encounter Count |
|---|---:|
| Low | 1–20 |
| Moderate | 21–60 |
| High | 61+ |

**Key Findings:**

| Utilization Group | Patients | Avg. Encounters per Patient | Avg. Cumulative Claim Cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 312 | 14.60 | $19,616.22 |
| Moderate | 1,052 | 36.30 | $107,212.43 |
| High | 388 | 155.86 | $428,350.10 |

A Pearson correlation analysis found a **moderate positive relationship (r = 0.454)** between patient encounter frequency and cumulative claim costs.

This suggests that patients with more encounters generally had higher cumulative costs, although encounter frequency alone did not explain all cost variation.

- Data quality checks and exploratory analysis

## Key Takeaways

This project strengthened my ability to work with relational healthcare data and translate analytical questions into SQL queries.

It also demonstrated the importance of validating data before analysis, understanding the difference between averages and cumulative totals, and interpreting statistical relationships carefully.

**Important Limitation:** Synthea produces synthetic data. Findings demonstrate SQL techniques and analytical reasoning but should not be interpreted as representative of real-world healthcare populations, utilization, or costs.
