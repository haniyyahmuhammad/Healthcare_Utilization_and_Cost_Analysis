

# Healthcare SQL Analysis

## Project Overview

An end-to-end healthcare analytics project using PostgreSQL and synthetic electronic health record (EHR) data generated by Synthea.

This project explores patient demographics, healthcare utilization, claim costs, clinical conditions, medication utilization, and payer coverage to identify patterns and support data-informed healthcare decisions.

### Project Objectives

- Build and validate a relational healthcare database.
- Analyze patient demographics and healthcare utilization.
- Identify healthcare cost and payer coverage patterns.
- Explore relationships between clinical conditions, utilization, and costs.
- Evaluate medication affordability and population reach.

## Dataset & Data Preparation

The dataset consists of synthetic healthcare records organized into four primary analytical tables.

| Table | Records |
|---|---:|
| Patients | 1,798 |
| Encounters | 103,214 |
| Conditions | 65,003 |
| Medications | 93,983 |

### Data Cleaning & Validation

- Used staging tables to preserve source data before transformation.
- Identified and repaired malformed CSV records using Python.
- Validated primary keys and patient-encounter relationships.
- Investigated missing values, duplicate records, and unmatched identifiers.
- Validated medication cost calculations and payer coverage values.
- Preserved unmatched medication references using data-quality flags.
- Recovered a missing medication reason description using a verified clinical code mapping.

Medication validation identified 72 records with unmatched patient references and 120 records with unmatched encounter references. These records were retained for medication-level analysis while unmatched patients were excluded from population-reach calculations.

## Analysis & Key Findings

### Phase 1: Database Setup & Data Preparation

- Configured PostgreSQL and pgAdmin.
- Generated and imported Synthea healthcare data.
- Established relational tables and validated data integrity.
- Developed repeatable data-cleaning and validation processes.

### Phase 2: Patient Population Analysis

Examined demographic characteristics and socioeconomic patterns.

- Total population: 1,798 patients.
- Gender distribution: approximately 51% female and 49% male.
- Approximately 82.5% of patients were identified as White.
- Patients aged 65 and older represented the largest age group (22.3%).
- Income and healthcare coverage showed virtually no linear correlation (r = -0.006).

### Phase 3: Healthcare Utilization Analysis

Examined encounter frequency, encounter types, and high-utilization patients.

- Total valid encounters: 103,214.
- Average encounters per patient with recorded encounters: 58.91.
- Median encounters: 36.
- Ambulatory encounters accounted for 54.1% of all encounters.
- Among the 20 highest-utilization patients, ambulatory encounters accounted for 86.9% of encounters.

**Key Insight:** High-utilization patients demonstrated different encounter patterns from the overall population.

### Phase 4: Healthcare Cost & Payer Coverage Analysis

Analyzed claim costs and payer contributions across encounter types.

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Total claim costs | $285.1M |
| Average claim cost per encounter | $2,762.30 |
| Median claim cost per encounter | $914.78 |
| Highest average encounter cost | Inpatient ($30,400.75) |
| Highest total encounter cost | Ambulatory ($160.4M) |

Payer coverage ranged from approximately 44.6% for wellness encounters to 81.5% for home encounters.

**Key Insight:** Encounter frequency and cost per encounter contribute differently to overall healthcare spending.

### Phase 5: Patient Utilization & Cost Analysis

Segmented patients with recorded encounters into exploratory utilization groups.

| Utilization Group | Patients | Avg. Encounters | Avg. Cumulative Claim Cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low (1–20) | 312 | 14.60 | $19,616.22 |
| Moderate (21–60) | 1,052 | 36.30 | $107,212.43 |
| High (61+) | 388 | 155.86 | $428,350.10 |

Pearson correlation between encounter frequency and cumulative claim costs:

**r = 0.454 — Moderate positive correlation**

**Key Insight:** Patients with more encounters generally accumulated higher claim costs, although utilization alone did not explain all cost variation.

### Phase 6: Clinical Analysis

#### 6.1 Clinical Conditions & Healthcare Utilization

Analyzed clinical condition records, disorder prevalence, and relationships between clinical complexity and healthcare utilization.

- Analyzed 65,003 condition records.
- Distinguished clinical disorders from other recorded findings and situations.
- Identified frequently recorded disorders and their patient reach.
- Examined the relationship between the number of distinct recorded disorders and encounter frequency.

Pearson correlation between recorded disorder count and encounter frequency:

**r = 0.632 — Positive correlation**

**Key Insight:** Patients with more recorded disorders tended to have more healthcare encounters. Further investigation could explore clinical complexity, care coordination, and utilization patterns.

Correlation does not establish causation.

#### 6.2 Medication Utilization & Affordability

Analyzed 93,983 medication records to evaluate medication utilization, payer coverage, recorded uncovered costs, and population reach.

##### Payer Coverage Distribution

| Coverage Category | Records | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Zero coverage | 31,299 | 33.30% |
| Partial coverage | 53,551 | 56.98% |
| Full coverage | 9,133 | 9.72% |

Approximately 90% of medication records had partial or zero payer coverage relative to the recorded base cost.

##### Medication Cost & Population Reach

| Medication | Records | Unique Patients | Population Reach | Avg. Uncovered Cost | Total Uncovered Cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Alteplase | 24 | 24 | 1.33% | $30,254.53 | $726,108.78 |
| Humulin | 7,959 | 120 | 6.67% | $71.93 | $572,457.04 |
| Sodium fluoride | 4,824 | 1,462 | 81.31% | $68.36 | $329,747.76 |
| Epoetin Alfa | 15,250 | 52 | 2.89% | $14.07 | $214,592.98 |
| Cisplatin | 4,343 | 23 | 1.28% | $41.72 | $181,208.09 |

**Key Insights:**

- Medication-record volume alone does not determine financial impact or population reach.
- Alteplase had the highest average and total recorded uncovered costs despite its low record volume.
- Sodium fluoride demonstrated broad population reach.
- Humulin combined substantial total uncovered costs with greater patient reach than Alteplase.

##### Healthcare Decision-Making

Medication affordability investigations should align with stakeholder objectives.

- **Broad population impact:** Investigate sodium fluoride.
- **High uncovered cost per record:** Investigate Alteplase.
- **Substantial cumulative uncovered costs and repeated medication records:** Investigate Humulin.

Before recommending coverage changes, additional analysis should evaluate clinical necessity, therapeutic alternatives, actual patient expenses, and barriers to medication access.

## Key Takeaways

- Data validation is essential before drawing analytical conclusions.
- Record volume, unique patient counts, average costs, and cumulative costs measure different aspects of healthcare activity.
- Higher utilization and greater clinical complexity are associated with increased healthcare activity and costs.
- Analytical recommendations should reflect stakeholder objectives, clinical context, and data limitations.

## Limitations

- Synthea generates synthetic healthcare data. Findings are not representative of actual healthcare populations or spending.
- Statistical correlations do not establish causation.
- Medication records do not necessarily represent prescriptions filled, administrations, or confirmed medication use.
- Recorded uncovered costs are calculated as base cost minus payer coverage. They should not be interpreted as confirmed patient out-of-pocket expenses or full costs across all dispenses.
- Medication-level cost calculations retain unmatched records, while patient-reach calculations include only matched patients.
- Population reach does not establish clinical necessity or financial hardship.
